  <p class="lede">The dashboard starts in 2012, with the VIIRS sensor. An older family of satellites, DMSP, has yearly night-time composites from 1992 to 2013. This page is a review of that older record for Indonesia: what it shows, where it can be trusted, and why it is not joined to the newer one here.</p>
</header>

<div class="notice">
  <p><strong>What this page can and cannot say.</strong> It shows three separate records: DMSP from 1992 to 2009, the DMSP satellite F18 from 2010 to 2013, and VIIRS from 2012. Nothing here ties one to the next, so no change across 2009 to 2010 or from DMSP to VIIRS can be read from it. DMSP numbers are not radiance. The shaded band is the typical disagreement between two satellites, not a confidence interval. None of this has been compared with electricity sales or any other record on the ground, so the page gives no growth figure for any place.</p>
</div>

const says = [];
{

  const mean = (list, key = "value") => d3.mean(list, (r) => r[key]);
  const early = before.filter((r) => r.year >= 1995 && r.year <= 1997), late = before.filter((r) => r.year >= 2005 && r.year <= 2007);
  const last3 = before.filter((r) => r.year >= 2007 && r.year <= 2009);
  const care = seen.filter(flagged).map((r) => r.year), gone = rows.filter((r) => r.gone).length, sat = summary[unit.i].saturated;
  if (!seen.length) says.push(html`<p>DMSP detected no light in <span class="dyn">${unit.name}</span> in any year from 1992 to 2013. That is "not detected", which is not the same as dark.</p>`);
  else {
    const flare = summary[unit.i].flare;
    if (flare !== null && flare >= 0.3) says.push(html`<p><span class="dyn">${pctNear(flare)}</span> of this place's DMSP light over the 22 years lies inside the outlines drawn around gas flares. Its line follows oil and gas as much as towns, so it cannot be read as settlements.</p>`);
    if (saturatedMost) says.push(html`<p>More than half of this place's light sits at the top of the DMSP scale in <span class="dyn">${sat}</span> of 22 years. A place like that cannot be seen to grow in DMSP, so no comparison between years is given.</p>`);
    else if (early.length === 3 && late.length === 3) {

  <h2>Why they are not joined here</h2>
  <p>Others have published one series from 1992 to 2018 by converting VIIRS radiance into a DMSP-like number. We tried the same kind of conversion on Indonesia for 2012 and 2013, the two years both sensors cover. It came close for the country as a whole and did not hold for provinces. DMSP has a footprint of about five kilometres and spreads each town's light over its surroundings; VIIRS sees the town itself. Small isolated towns carry more of their DMSP light in that surrounding glow, which is the likely reason Nusa Tenggara, Sulawesi and Maluku come out too low in the conversion. It does not explain Papua, where the conversion comes out far too high and the fit is worst.</p>
