Palu 2018: the light that took weeks to return

At 18:02 on 28 September 2018 a magnitude 7.5 earthquake struck 72 km north of Palu. This page follows the lit cells of Palu, Sigi and Donggala night by night for a month, and sets the result beside Lombok.

What this page can and cannot say. Nothing here has been checked against records of power or damage. Cloud hid Palu on most nights, the control cities are hundreds of kilometres away and seen from other angles, and only the part of the affected area south of the equator and west of 120 E is covered. The figures against a similar viewing angle rest on two or three reference nights, too few to test that method here. The page says how much light was seen. It does not say why a place was dark, and nothing here is a statement about electricity.

2 · What only the comparison shows

Weeks, where Lombok took a night

After Lombok's third earthquake the cells seen lost about half their light and had it back the next night. Palu was still far down three nights later, and well down ten nights later. The level of single nights cannot be trusted here: the same town reads tens of percent brighter or dimmer as the satellite's angle changes, and before the earthquake Palu already ranged from to of its reference. But every sixteenth night the angle repeats. Six nights before the earthquake, ten nights after and twenty-six nights after are such a set. Ten nights after, the cells seen on both nights gave of their light before the earthquake. Twenty-six nights after, about . A wider rule gives more nights: compare each cell with any reference night seen from within degrees of the same angle. By that rule the area stood at on the night of the earthquake, ten nights after and at twenty-six, with the nights from the thirteenth on ranging from to on whichever cells the cloud allowed. So on the two nights that can be compared at one angle the area stood at about six tenths and then about nine tenths. Each of those is a single night, and nothing in between is a clean curve.

A cell can only be counted on a night it was seen, so the counts seen back are lower bounds and the nights are upper bounds. This count still divides by the distant control cities, and the rule that calls a cell dimmed also catches some cells on ordinary nights, so read the map for where light was seen back, not for exact dates.

4 · What stayed invisible

Most nights, the satellite saw cloud

This is the hardest of the four case studies to read, and the reasons belong on the page. The area was seen well on only a handful of nights. The epicentre is in this tile, 72 km north of the city, but the parts of Donggala and Parigi Moutong north of the equator are in another tile and are not here. No dark cell within 40 km of the epicentre became lit; the city lies outside that radius, so this says nothing about the city. And a cell that was never seen back cannot be told apart from one that was simply under cloud on every later night.
