How to read a status. A status says how a unit's light compares with its own past. It does not say why: this page makes no claim about electricity, outages or the economy, and none of it has been checked against records on the ground.

A cell is about 460 m across but the sensor's view of the ground is wider, so read patterns rather than single cells. Outlines are simplified for display: near a border, a lit cell can be drawn in the neighbouring unit (about 2% of cells at sub-district level), while the figures use the exact boundaries.

Reading this page

What the statuses mean
  • Normal: the unit's light is within what its own past explains.
  • Watch and Alarm: the unit is dimmer than its own past explains, and clearly dimmer than the rest of its district or province. The two lines are set to be crossed in about 5% and 1% of months. Measured over 2018 to 2026 they were crossed in 5.1% and 1.1 to 1.3% of months across all districts and sub-districts, and in 5.7 to 5.8% and 1.3 to 1.4% across the ones that can be judged at their own level; single years ran from 0.1% to 3.4% at the alarm line. Those are crossings of the unit's own line; with the comparison against the surrounding area added, Watch or Alarm was shown in about 2% of judged months and Alarm in 0.3 to 0.4%. Where the comparison with the rest of the district or province is not available, the unit's own past decides alone, and the text under the map says so.
  • Regional dimming: the unit is dimmer than its own past explains, but not clearly dimmer than the rest of its district or province. Year-to-year movement shared across an area looks like this. Smoke haze is a possible cause in some months, but that has not been checked against a fire or aerosol record. For a province no comparison is made, so a province only ever shows this status, never Watch or Alarm.
  • Low confidence (striped on the map): a watch, alarm or regional reading on a unit with fewer than lit cells, or one whose readings vary too much from month to month, or one for which that is not yet measured. Read the district or province above it instead.
  • Seen, no light: enough of the unit was seen and none of its usable cells showed detectable light. The product stores radiance under 0.5 nW cm-2 sr-1 as zero. Nearly all such readings since 2018 (2,262 of 2,364 at sub-district level) are in units of fewer than 20 lit cells.
  • Not judged: the unit was seen, but too few of its earlier months were observed, overall or in that calendar month, for an expected value, or there is not yet enough history for thresholds. A monitor has no alarm line before for districts and sub-districts, and provinces have no line before .
  • Not observed: under half of the unit's usual light sat in cells with a usable value. This is never the same as dark.
What else the map can be coloured by
  • Against expected: this month's light as a percentage of what up to six of the unit's own previous years lead one to expect. It is the number the statuses are judged from, without the judgement.
  • Against usual: this month's light as a percentage of the unit's usual level over the whole record. It shows level and growth, not what is unusual.
  • How well seen: the share of the unit's usual light that sat in cells with a usable value. It decides what every other figure is worth.
  • Months reading low: how many months of its current spell a unit has been in Watch or Alarm. Regional dimming is not counted. A normal month, or a month seen with no light, ends a spell, and so do months in a row without a reading; shorter gaps are skipped. Four spells in five last one month. Shown for units of at least lit cells. The count is about the unit as a whole, not about each place in it.
  • Newly lit cells: each lit cell is classed by its typical yearly radiance in to and in to : lit at both ends, brightened (dimly lit then, lit now), emerged (dark then, lit now) or faded. Dark is under nW cm-2 sr-1 and lit is at least . A unit is filled by the share of its cells that emerged. Across the country about 2% of lit cells emerged and about 39% brightened. Lit area is the area of the lit cells, about 0.21 km2 each near the equator.
  • Trend against the median: the rate of change of the unit's light over to , in percent a year: the median slope between every pair of yearly values (Theil-Sen), where a year's value is the median of its months and needs at least of them, and a unit needs at least such years and lit cells. The range is a % interval widened for the way one year resembles the next. Units are coloured by whether that range lies above or below the median unit of their level. A second figure in the text counts only cells that were already lit in : it is lower, because the first includes cells that are counted only because they grew.

These rules were revised after a critique on 2026-11-23. None has been checked against events or records on the ground.

What the lit cells can show

The points on the map are the lit cells, each about 460 m across. "Lit cells show" changes what they are coloured by, and the month slider moves them.

  • Usual light: the cell's mean radiance over the record. It does not change with the month.
  • Radiance this month: the cell's radiance in classes, from no detectable light to nW cm-2 sr-1 or more. Only values the product marks good quality with at least observations are used; other cells are grey.
  • Change from a year earlier: this month's radiance over the same calendar month a year earlier. It removes the time of year but not growth: where lights are growing, brighter than a year ago is the ordinary state.
  • Observations this month: how many observations stand behind the month's value. It shows why a cell is grey in the other layers.

  • Then and now: the cell's light in to against to : lit at both ends, brightened from dim, emerged from dark, or faded. It does not change with the month.

These describe single cells. No status is judged from them, and the sensor's view of the ground is wider than one cell, so neighbouring cells are not independent: read patterns, not single pixels. Each layer has a switch beside its menu: turn the unit fills off to read the cells on their own, or the cells off to read the fills.

What the figures rest on
  • Source: NASA Black Marble monthly composites (VNP46A3, Collection 2), all view angles. A cell's monthly value is used when the product marks it good quality and it rests on at least observations. No step removes fires or gas flares as such: a persistent flare stays in.
  • Lit cells: land cells inside a coded admin unit whose mean radiance over their good-quality months is at least nW cm-2 sr-1, with at least such months: in all.
  • Expected light: fitted to up to six of the unit's own previous years (at least 24 observed months), allowing for growth and for the time of year. Each month is judged with thresholds read from earlier years only.
  • Judged at its own level: a unit with at least lit cells for which a one-month drop of % or less would cross its alarm line four times in five.
  • Months: to . About 40% of unit-months are not observed.
  • What this page cannot say: why a unit is dim. A one-month composite also hides short events: a drop that lasts days can leave the month looking normal. A unit's figure describes its lit cells taken together, not each place in it.
  • Outlines are simplified for display. No figure is computed from them.
